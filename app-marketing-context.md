## Value proposition

- **Problem:** Useful screenshots, saved social posts, links, photos, and notes
  pile up across mobile apps, then become difficult to find or use again.
- **Target audience:** **Hypothesis:** visually minded iPhone users who save
  frequently from Instagram, X, Safari, Photos, and Notes but do not maintain a
  folder system.
- **Unique differentiator:** One capture point for links, images, and notes;
  Shelvr understands each save, files it into a Space, and makes it searchable.
- **Elevator pitch:** Save anything in one tap; Shelvr organizes it so it is
  useful when you need it.
- **Emotional promise:** Less organizing, more remembering.

## Jobs and pain language

The onboarding survey provides the initial segmentation vocabulary:

- Pileups: X bookmarks, Instagram Saved, screenshots, Notes, browser tabs,
  and “everywhere.” Browser tabs are a secondary segment to validate, not a
  lead acquisition angle for the mobile app.
- Common saves: articles, recipes, products, home and decor, travel, fitness,
  inspiration, and videos.
- Desired outcome: get something out of the inbox or camera roll and be able to
  find it later without manual upkeep.

## Competitive landscape

These are positioning hypotheses, not a completed competitive audit.

| Alternative | What it does well | Shelvr opening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browser bookmarks / reading-list apps | Familiar and fast for links | Links only; weak for screenshots, notes, and visual recall |
| Photos / screenshot albums | Already part of the capture habit | Manual cleanup and poor cross-format organization |
| Notes apps | Flexible and searchable | Saving is manual and metadata is inconsistent |
| Pinterest / social saved folders | Strong discovery and visual collections | Content remains fragmented by platform |
| Raindrop.io / MyMind-style tools | Cross-format saving and polished organization | Win on mobile capture simplicity, useful Spaces, and calm positioning |

## Goals and KPIs

### 30-day organic sprint

1. Find one content format that repeatedly reaches qualified viewers — target:
   at least three posts above the account's rolling median and one post above
   10,000 qualified views.
2. Prove that content creates intent — target: measurable profile visits,
   tracked App Store clicks, and first opens by content angle.
3. Find the strongest pain segment — target: one segment with materially better
   click-to-activation performance than the other tested segments.

### Product metrics

- Primary activation: onboarding completed, followed by a first save within
  seven days.
- First-save events: `article_saved`, `note_saved`, `images_saved`,
  `photo_captured`, or `shared_content_saved`.
- Quality guardrails: suggestion acceptance, fast deletion rate, and retention.
- Acquisition diagnostics: video views, completion rate, comments, profile
  visits, bio-link taps, App Store page views, downloads, onboarding completion,
  and first save.

Views alone are not a success metric. A smaller post with activated users beats
a viral post with no product intent.
